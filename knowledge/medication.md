# Medication

_General guidance, not veterinary advice. Check with your vet for your pet's specific needs._

## Giving medication
Give medication at the same times each day, and follow the vet's dose and duration exactly. Do not stop early, even if your pet seems better. Doses depend on weight and age, so puppies, kittens and seniors often need different amounts than adults. Never use a dose from the internet or from another pet.

## Common routines
Many pets need monthly flea, tick and heartworm prevention from a young age, and some need daily pills for long-term conditions. Set a reminder on a calendar or in PawPal+ so doses are not missed.

## Missed doses
If you miss a dose, check the label or call your vet. Do not double up on the next dose unless instructed.

## Safety
Never give human medication to pets without a vet's approval. Many common ones, such as ibuprofen and acetaminophen, are toxic, and some are especially dangerous to cats. Contact a vet right away if your pet shows a bad reaction, such as vomiting, swelling, trouble breathing or collapse.

## Storage
Store medication as the label directs, out of reach of pets and children, in its original container. Keep a list of every medication and supplement your pet takes to share at vet visits.

## Pilling tips
Hide a pill in a small piece of soft food only if your vet confirms the medication can be given with food. Follow with water or a treat, and check the pet has swallowed it. For liquid medicine, aim into the cheek pouch slowly rather than the back of the throat.

## Medication for young and old pets
Young animals are still developing, so ask your vet before giving any product to a puppy or kitten under 8 weeks. Older pets may be more sensitive to side effects and may need regular blood tests while on long-term medication.
