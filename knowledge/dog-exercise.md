# Dog Exercise

_General guidance, not veterinary advice. Check with your vet for your pet's specific needs._

## Daily walks
Most healthy adult dogs need 30 to 60 minutes of exercise per day, usually split into two or more walks. High-energy breeds such as retrievers, collies, terriers and many working breeds may need up to two hours. Flat-faced breeds such as bulldogs and pugs need shorter, calmer walks.

## Puppies (under about 12 months)
Puppies need short, frequent play sessions rather than long walks. A common rule is about 5 minutes of structured exercise per month of age, up to twice a day, so a 4-month-old puppy gets roughly 20 minutes per session. Avoid long runs, repeated jumping and forced exercise on hard surfaces while the joints and growth plates are developing. Free play on grass, short training games and gentle socialization walks are ideal. Large and giant breeds mature more slowly and should be kept to gentle exercise for longer.

## Adolescent dogs (about 6 to 18 months)
Adolescents have lots of energy but are still growing. Build up gradually to 30 to 60 minutes a day with a mix of walks, fetch, and training. Small and medium breeds are usually physically mature by about 12 months, while large breeds may take 18 to 24 months. A 1-year-old dog often needs more exercise than an adult and benefits from structured training to burn off mental energy.

## Adult dogs (1 year and older)
A healthy adult dog, including a 1-year-old, needs about 30 to 60 minutes of walks and exercise a day. Keep a steady routine. Weekend-warrior exercise, such as a long hike after a week of short walks, raises the risk of injury. Increase distance gradually, over several weeks.

## Mental stimulation
Puzzle toys, training sessions and sniff walks tire a dog out as much as physical exercise. Aim for 10 to 15 minutes of enrichment daily. Rotate toys to keep them interesting.

## Weather and safety
Walk in the cooler parts of the day in summer, carry water, and stop if your dog pants heavily, lags behind or seeks shade. Check pavement with the back of your hand. Signs of overexertion include heavy panting that does not settle, limping, vomiting and collapse. Contact a vet right away if these occur.

## Older dogs
Senior dogs still need daily movement but in shorter, gentler sessions. See the senior pets guide.
