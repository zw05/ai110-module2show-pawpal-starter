# Travel and Home Safety

_General guidance, not veterinary advice. Check with your vet for your pet's specific needs._

## Car travel
Use a secured carrier or a crash-tested harness. Never leave a pet in a parked car, because temperatures can reach dangerous levels within minutes. Offer water and a break every 2 to 3 hours on long trips. Young puppies and kittens can get carsick, so start with short trips and avoid feeding a large meal beforehand.

## Hot and cold weather
In hot weather, walk early or late, carry water and check pavement with the back of your hand. In cold weather, shorten walks and dry paws afterward. Short-nosed breeds, puppies, seniors and thin-coated dogs are more sensitive to heat and cold. Signs of heatstroke include heavy panting, drooling, weakness and vomiting, so move the pet to shade, cool it with water and call a vet immediately.

## Safety by age
- **Puppies and kittens:** explore with their mouths, so remove cords, small objects and plants. Supervise around stairs and windows.
- **Adults:** keep a secure fence or leash, and a recall command for off-leash areas.
- **Seniors:** may slip on floors and struggle with heat, cold and stairs. Add rugs and ramps.

## Toxic items at home
Chocolate, xylitol (a sweetener in some gum and peanut butter), grapes, onions, lilies (for cats) and many household plants are poisonous. Antifreeze, rat poison and human medication are also dangerous. If your pet eats something toxic, call a vet or a pet poison hotline right away.

## Travel and boarding
Plan ahead with up-to-date vaccines and a copy of records. Bring familiar food, bedding and medication. Airlines and hotels have their own pet rules, so check them early.

## ID
Keep a collar tag and a registered microchip up to date with your current phone number. Take a recent photo of your pet in case of loss.

## Emergencies
Know the location and phone number of your nearest emergency vet. Seek urgent care for trouble breathing, seizures, collapse, repeated vomiting, bloated belly, heavy bleeding or suspected poisoning.
