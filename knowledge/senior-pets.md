# Senior Pets

_General guidance, not veterinary advice. Check with your vet for your pet's specific needs._

## When is a pet senior
Small dogs are generally considered senior from about 8 to 10 years old, medium dogs from about 7 to 8 years, and large and giant breeds from about 5 to 6 years. Cats are considered senior from about 10 years old.

## Life stages in brief
Puppies and kittens grow quickly in their first year. Young adults, from about 1 to 3 years old, are energetic. Mature adults, from about 3 to 7 years, are in their prime and settle into routines. Seniors need extra attention to comfort and health.

## Exercise for senior dogs
Senior dogs still need daily walks, but shorter and gentler. Try two or three 15 to 20 minute walks a day instead of one long one, and avoid hard running or jumping. Swimming and slow sniff walks are good low-impact options.

## Care changes
Senior pets often need more frequent vet checkups, around every 6 months, softer bedding, easy access to food and water, and a diet suited to their age. Ramps or steps help pets who struggle with stairs or furniture. Use non-slip rugs on slippery floors.

## Signs to watch for
Watch for weight loss or gain, drinking much more than usual, stiffness, limping, confusion, accidents indoors, bad breath, lumps and changes in appetite or sleep. Many of these can be treated if caught early, so tell your vet.

## Comfort and enrichment
Keep routines steady, provide warm and quiet resting places, and offer gentle play, puzzle feeders and short training games to keep the mind active. Trim nails regularly and brush gently.

## Medication
Seniors are more likely to take regular medication. Give it on schedule and keep a list for your vet. See the medication guide.
