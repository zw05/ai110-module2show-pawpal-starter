# Grooming

_General guidance, not veterinary advice. Check with your vet for your pet's specific needs._

## Brushing
Short-coated dogs need brushing about weekly. Long or thick coats need brushing several times a week to prevent mats. Short-haired cats need brushing about once a week, and long-haired cats every day or two. Brushing also lets you check the skin for lumps, ticks and sore spots.

## Grooming by age
- **Puppies and kittens:** start handling paws, ears, mouth and the brush early, in very short, rewarding sessions, so grooming is never scary later.
- **Adults:** follow a regular schedule for brushing, nails, ears and teeth.
- **Seniors:** may need more help because of stiffness. Groom in shorter sessions on a soft surface, and watch for new lumps, matting or sore skin.

## Bathing
Most dogs need a bath every 4 to 8 weeks, or when dirty. Use a shampoo made for dogs, since human shampoo can dry the skin. Puppies can have a gentle bath once they are old enough, usually after the first vaccines. Cats rarely need baths.

## Nail trimming
Trim nails every 3 to 4 weeks, or when you hear clicking on hard floors. Cut only the tip and avoid the pink quick. If you cut it, apply styptic powder and press gently. Overgrown nails can make walking painful.

## Ears and eyes
Check ears weekly for redness, odor or discharge. Wipe the outer ear with a vet-approved cleaner and never push anything into the ear canal. Wipe away eye discharge with a clean damp cloth. See a vet for head shaking, heavy discharge, squinting or red eyes.

## Teeth
Brush teeth several times a week, ideally daily, with pet-safe toothpaste. See the dental care guide.

## Shedding and professional grooming
Many dogs shed more in spring and autumn. Regular brushing reduces hair around the house. Breeds with continuously growing coats, such as poodles, need professional grooming every 4 to 8 weeks.
