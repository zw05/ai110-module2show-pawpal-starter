# Cat Care

_General guidance, not veterinary advice. Check with your vet for your pet's specific needs._

## Life stages
- **Kittens (up to 1 year):** grow fast, need frequent meals, lots of sleep and play, and a vaccine series. See the puppy and kitten guide.
- **Adults (1 to about 10 years):** need a steady routine, two meals a day, daily play and yearly checkups. A 1-year-old cat is usually considered a young adult and is still very playful.
- **Seniors (about 10 years and older):** need checkups about every 6 months, softer bedding and easy access to litter boxes. See the senior pets guide.

## Feeding
Adult cats usually eat two measured meals a day. Wet food adds helpful hydration. Kittens eat three or four small meals a day. See the cat feeding guide for details.

## Play
Kittens and young cats benefit from 15 to 30 minutes of active play split into several short sessions a day. Adult cats need 10 to 20 minutes, once or twice a day, using wand toys or chasing games. This helps prevent boredom, destructive behavior and weight gain. Let your cat "catch" the toy at the end of play. Older cats like slower, gentler games.

## Walks for cats
Cats do not need daily walks the way dogs do. Indoor play and climbing cover their exercise needs. Some cats can be trained to walk in a secure harness on a leash outdoors: start indoors, let the cat explore at its own pace, and keep outings short, around 10 to 15 minutes at first. Never force a cat to walk, never use a collar alone, and make sure vaccines and flea and tick prevention are up to date before going outside. Kittens should not go out until their vaccine series is complete.

## Litter box
Scoop at least once a day and fully change the litter weekly. Provide one box per cat plus one extra, in quiet places away from food and water. Kittens need a low-sided box. Sudden changes such as going outside the box, straining, or frequent trips with little urine can signal a urinary problem. Male cats that cannot urinate need emergency care.

## Scratching and enrichment
Provide sturdy scratching posts, ideally tall enough for a full stretch, along with window perches and hiding spots. Rotate toys. Indoor cats need extra enrichment to stay active.

## Grooming
Short-haired cats need brushing about once a week. Long-haired cats need brushing every day or two to prevent mats. See the grooming guide.

## Health basics
Keep up with vaccines, parasite prevention and yearly vet visits. Hiding, not eating for more than a day, vomiting repeatedly or breathing with an open mouth are reasons to call a vet.
