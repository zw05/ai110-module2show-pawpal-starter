# Fleas, Ticks and Parasites

_General guidance, not veterinary advice. Check with your vet for your pet's specific needs._

## Prevention
Use year-round flea, tick and heartworm prevention recommended by your vet. Products made for dogs can be toxic to cats, so never use a product on a different species than its label says. Check the label for minimum age and weight, since many products are not safe for very young puppies and kittens.

## Parasite care by age
- **Puppies and kittens:** can be born with roundworms, so they are dewormed every 2 weeks from about 2 weeks old until 12 weeks, then monthly until 6 months, according to your vet's plan. Start flea and heartworm prevention at the age on the label.
- **Adults:** keep monthly prevention going all year, and test for heartworm and intestinal parasites at the yearly checkup.
- **Seniors:** are often less able to cope with parasites and illness, so keep prevention up to date.

## Checking for ticks
After walks in grass or woods, run your hands over your pet, especially around the ears, neck, armpits and between the toes. Remove a tick with fine tweezers close to the skin, pulling straight out, and clean the area. Do not use heat, oil or nail polish.

## Fleas
Fleas live mostly in the home, not on the pet, so treat the pet and the environment. Wash bedding in hot water, vacuum often and continue treatment for several months.

## Heartworm and intestinal worms
Heartworm is spread by mosquitoes and is far easier to prevent than treat. Intestinal worms can pass to people, especially children, so pick up stool promptly and wash hands.

## Signs of trouble
Constant scratching, small black specks in the coat, scooting, a swollen belly, weight loss, coughing or worms in stool call for a vet visit.
