# Dog Feeding

_General guidance, not veterinary advice. Check with your vet for your pet's specific needs._

## Meal schedule by age
- **Puppies under 6 months:** three or four small meals a day.
- **Puppies 6 to 12 months:** two or three meals a day.
- **Adults (about 1 year and older):** two meals a day, roughly 8 to 12 hours apart.
- **Seniors:** two smaller meals are usually easier on digestion.

Small breeds are adults by about 9 to 12 months, and large breeds by 18 to 24 months. Move from puppy to adult food around those ages, switching gradually over 5 to 7 days.

## Puppy food
Feed a complete and balanced food labeled for growth. Large-breed puppies should eat a large-breed puppy formula, which controls calcium and calories so bones grow at a steady rate. Do not give extra calcium supplements unless your vet advises it.

## Portions
Follow the food label as a starting point and adjust to keep your dog lean. You should be able to feel, but not see, the ribs, and see a visible waist from above. Weigh food rather than scooping when possible, and recheck the amount as your puppy grows or your dog's activity changes. Spayed or neutered dogs often need fewer calories.

## Water
Fresh water should always be available. Rinse and refill the bowl daily. A puppy may drink and urinate more often, which is normal.

## Treats and weight
Treats should be no more than about 10 percent of daily calories. Training treats can be tiny pieces. If you cannot easily feel your dog's ribs, or they have no visible waist, talk to your vet about reducing portions.

## Foods dogs should never eat
Chocolate, grapes and raisins, onions, garlic, macadamia nuts, xylitol (a sweetener in some gum, candy and peanut butter), alcohol and cooked bones are dangerous. Call a vet or a pet poison hotline right away if your dog eats any of them.

## Feeding habits
Feed at consistent times in a quiet spot. Wait about an hour after a big meal before hard exercise, especially for deep-chested breeds, because of the risk of bloat. A dog that suddenly stops eating for more than a day, or a puppy that refuses meals, should be checked by a vet.
