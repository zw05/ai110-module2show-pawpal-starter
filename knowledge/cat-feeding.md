# Cat Feeding

_General guidance, not veterinary advice. Check with your vet for your pet's specific needs._

## Meals by age
- **Kittens under 6 months:** three to four small meals a day of food labeled for growth.
- **Kittens 6 to 12 months:** two to three meals a day. Switch to adult food at about 12 months, gradually over 5 to 7 days.
- **Adults:** two meals a day. Measure portions using the food label as a starting point and adjust to keep a healthy weight.
- **Seniors:** may need smaller, more frequent meals, or a senior formula. Weight loss in an older cat should be checked by a vet.

## Wet and dry food
Wet food adds water, which supports urinary and kidney health. Many owners feed a mix. Cats are obligate carnivores, so choose a complete and balanced food made for cats. Dog food is not suitable for cats.

## Water
Keep fresh water available at all times. Many cats drink more from a wide bowl, a fountain or several spots around the home, away from the litter box and the food bowl.

## Weight
You should be able to feel the ribs under a thin layer of fat and see a tucked waist. Overweight cats are at higher risk of diabetes and joint problems. Reduce weight slowly. Never put a cat on a crash diet, because rapid weight loss can cause serious liver disease.

## Foods to avoid
Onions, garlic, chocolate, grapes and raisins, alcohol, caffeine and raw dough are toxic to cats. Lilies are extremely dangerous. Milk can upset many cats' stomachs.

## Feeding habits
Feed in a quiet spot. Puzzle feeders slow down eating and give indoor cats something to do. A cat that stops eating for more than 24 hours needs a vet check.
