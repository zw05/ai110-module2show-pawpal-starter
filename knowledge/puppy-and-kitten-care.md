# Puppy and Kitten Care

_General guidance, not veterinary advice. Check with your vet for your pet's specific needs._

## Vaccines and checkups
Young pets need a series of vaccines starting at about 6 to 8 weeks, usually repeated every 3 to 4 weeks until about 16 weeks. A booster is typically given at about 1 year old, and then on the schedule your vet sets. Your vet will also advise on deworming, microchipping, and spay or neuter timing.

## Growth stages
- **Newborn to 8 weeks:** stays with the mother and littermates. Weaning begins at about 4 weeks.
- **8 to 16 weeks:** the key socialization window. Pets go to new homes and begin training and vaccines.
- **4 to 6 months:** teething, with adult teeth replacing baby teeth.
- **6 to 12 months:** adolescence, with energy, testing limits and sexual maturity.
- **About 1 year:** small and medium dogs and most cats are close to adult size. Large and giant dogs keep growing until 18 to 24 months.

## Feeding
Feed a food labeled for growth. Puppies and kittens eat 3 to 4 small meals a day until about 6 months, then move to 2 meals. Switch foods gradually over 5 to 7 days. Weigh your pet regularly to track steady growth.

## Sleep and rest
Puppies and kittens sleep 16 to 20 hours a day. Give them a quiet place to rest and do not wake them for play. Overtired young pets can become cranky and bitey.

## Socialization
Between about 3 and 14 weeks, gently introduce new people, sounds, surfaces and handling. Keep experiences positive and short, and only visit places safe for unvaccinated puppies until your vet says otherwise.

## Exercise and play
Keep play short and frequent. Puppies need about 5 minutes of structured exercise per month of age, up to twice a day. Kittens like short bursts of wand-toy play. See the dog exercise guide.

## Safety at home
Puppy-proof and kitten-proof the home. Hide electrical cords, keep small objects, medication, plants and cleaning products out of reach, and check that windows and balconies are secure.

## Teething and chewing
Offer safe chew toys. Frozen carrots or chilled rubber toys can soothe gums. Contact your vet if baby teeth have not fallen out by about 7 months.
